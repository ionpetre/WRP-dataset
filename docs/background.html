How similar are the cluster shares?

In each survey, people at opposite ends of the income and education scales were distributed across the five clusters in broadly similar proportions.

How to read the overlap

In each country we take the smaller of the two groups’ shares for each cluster and add the five values. A score of 100% means the two groups have identical shares. We then average across countries, giving each country equal weight.

Some worries are more common at lower incomes

Who experienced the harm matters

Personal harm means harm to the respondent. Known-person harm means harm to someone they know. In every survey, people in the lowest income quintile more often reported personal harm and less often reported harm to someone known. Known-person harm was also reported less often by the group with primary education or less.

Background gives limited clues about an individual’s cluster

On 2025 respondents from countries excluded from classifier training, a model using 18 background variables reached 40.71% weighted accuracy. Predicting the most common cluster gave 36.92%. The direct comparisons above show how membership is distributed across groups.
