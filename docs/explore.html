Meet the five worry–harm clusters

Each cluster brings together people with similar answers about worry and reported harm. All five recurred in 2021, 2023 and 2025.

  • External hazardsVery high worry about traffic, crime and severe weather is common. Water and mental-health worries are less prominent.
  • Basic necessitiesWater and food stand out. In all three surveys, a large majority of this cluster were very worried about each.
  • Mental-health worryAlmost everyone in this cluster is very worried about mental health. Very high worry about traffic is also common.
  • Harm to people they knowMembers often report serious harm to someone they know across several areas of life.
  • Few very high worriesMembers rarely choose “very worried” across the six topics. Food can still be a very high worry for some members.

How to read the rules and percentages

The answer rule describes the cluster. Precision is the share of rule matches who belong to the cluster. Coverage is the share of cluster members who match the rule. The model assigns each complete respondent to one cluster. A person can meet several rules or meet none.

Cluster sizes and answer distributions use each wave’s own development sample with equal total weight per country. Rule scores compare the rules on the same set of 92 countries in all three waves.

Evidence across surveys

We checked whether the same five clusters recur across years and when we vary the people and countries included in the analysis.

Every pair of surveys supports the same five clusters

We fitted each survey independently and let each fit classify the same set of complete survey answers. The fitted labels agreed for about 97–98% of the weighted respondents in every pair.

Adjusted Rand index (ARI) is 1 when two partitions agree exactly and near 0 for chance-level agreement. Weighted agreement gives the share with matching labels after we align the clusters.

What happens when we repeat the fitting?

We refitted each survey on smaller samples of respondents or countries. Each cluster passed the recovery criterion in every survey pair and under both checks.

Each cross-wave ARI range covers the middle 80% of 2,500 comparisons. Cluster recovery uses weighted Jaccard overlap, the share of the combined membership present in both fits. Each score is the least favourable 10th percentile across wave pairs and both types of refit.
