Methods and interpretation

The website presents the paper’s final clustering results. The complete companion notebook contains editable analytical code, saved results and the full acceptable set.

The main findings

Full worry–harm models use six worries and fourteen personal/known-person harm indicators in 2021, 2023 and 2025. The final seven-cluster models have point ARIs of 0.95–0.96 and matched weighted agreement of 97.20–97.89%. Water/food, mental-health, broad known-person-harm and lower-severe-worry clusters form a stronger recurring core, covering 69.90% of the common 2025 reference weight. Traffic, crime and severe-weather divisions vary more under refitting.

A separate five-worry analysis uses food, water, crime, severe weather and mental health. The final later-wave three-cluster point functions agree on all 243 possible complete answer patterns. Between 92.12% and 98.00% of cross-refit pairs agree exactly, but rare alternative fits differ substantially. Retaining all nine passing 2019 settings did not establish a robust shared partition including 2019. Its displayed model is a separate within-wave alternative.

Independent fitting and later joint selection

Each wave has its own development-country frame, worry coding q, worry–harm allocation α where applicable, and independently fitted K-means centres. All accepted settings satisfy the original size and global-stability filters. We retained 31 full settings and 16 five-worry settings.

At each common cluster count, compare every set with one accepted model per wave on the same 2025 reference respondents. Rank by the minimum pairwise ARI, then mean ARI, then ordered setting identifiers. This is joint development selection after independent fitting. The original full centres favour seven clusters; previously saved common-country sensitivity refits favour six. The paper reports this sensitivity. No original threshold was relaxed and no new held-out assessment is attached to the final jointly selected models.

Eligibility, coding and weights

Keep complete substantive answers for the selected input family, without imputation. Source worry codes very/somewhat/not become 2/1/0. For harm, personal only gives (1,0), known-person only (0,1), both (1,1), and neither (0,0). Unknown, refused and missing answers remain missing. The two indicators concern different recipients.

Code worry as 0/q/1, subtract the person’s mean and retain that mean as a reduced-influence coordinate. Full models scale these seven worry coordinates by √(α/6) and all fourteen harm indicators by √((1−α)/14). Five-worry models use their centred values and mean divided by √5. Weighted K-means minimizes weighted squared Euclidean distance. Every country has equal total fitting/reporting mass, retaining relative WGT within eligible country populations. These are not world-population shares.

Comparing definitions and frequencies

Definition comparisons apply both models to identical reference answers: 90,395 full respondents in 92 common development countries, or 91,249 five-worry respondents in 90. Ordinary ARI uses respondent counts and is not percentage accuracy. Weighted agreement uses optimal one-to-one matching. Each overlap-table row uses country/survey weights and sums to 100% before rounding.

The repeated-fit view reuses the original fifty respondent and fifty country confirmations on each setting’s native development frame. Country subsampling retains approximately 80% of countries, stratified by region and income. Each refit assigns the same reference respondents. Fifty runs give 1,225 distinct within-wave pairs and 2,500 cross-wave pairs. The displayed ranges are p10–p90 and the dot is the median. They are conditional dependent distributions, not confidence intervals.

Frequency comparisons instead keep the final 2025 model fixed and assign each wave’s own respondents in the common country set. They use the final seven-cluster full definition and final three-cluster worry definition. Differences are percentage points. Independent cross-sections do not identify individual transitions.

Rule descriptions

Every cluster receives one Boolean description with condition cost at most three and precision at least 80%. Find the maximum attainable coverage, then choose minimum cost within one percentage point of it. Break ties by precision, coverage, original-question count and canonical ordered DNF. The code enumerates the entire stated language: 1,491,084 full-model formulas or 134,200 five-worry formulas.

Precision is the weighted fraction of rule matches belonging to the cluster. Coverage is the weighted fraction of cluster members matching it. K and P count known-person and personal harm domains out of seven. V, N and A count very, not and at-least-somewhat worried answers across six or five domains. A count threshold has cost one but needs its underlying questions. Rules may overlap or leave people uncovered, and their precision need not transfer unchanged to another wave.

Country maps and distances

Country views use the final selected models and only their native development countries. They compare national cluster mixtures by Hellinger distance, or original question marginals by total variation. Full original-answer distance uses the selected model’s allocation α. Each colour denominator is the median over distinct development-country pairs under the final 2025 model of the same input family. It stays fixed when another wave is shown.

The cluster-similarity overview projects square-root shares into two principal coordinates. Exact nearest-country ranks use the full distance. Colour axes are specific to a model, and country coverage can differ across waves. Income-group summaries are descriptive associations among reused country pairs. No old benchmark uncertainty or held-out result is copied onto a new model.

Code, data and alternatives

The paper companion notebook uses NumPy, pandas, SciPy, scikit-learn and Matplotlib. Run All verifies the saved analyses without fitting. Optional complete fitting and exhaustive rule extraction are visible inline. The historical adaptive proposal catalogue is supplied as data, avoiding a specialist optimizer dependency.

The original World Risk Poll data are supplied separately by Lloyd’s Register Foundation. This site contains aggregate data only. Historical benchmarks and prediction experiments are retained as a labelled repository archive, outside the published visualization. Their definitions and evidence are not substitutes for the final models.
